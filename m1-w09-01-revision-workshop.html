Lesson 9.1 · Revision Workshop
Making the Draft Better, Not Just Neater
Turning your Week 8 draft into the final essay
🎯 OBJECTIVE
By the end of this lesson you will be able to revise your essay at three levels — argument, paragraph, and sentence — using a checklist and your feedback, so the final version is genuinely stronger than the draft, not just cleaner.
📚 REVISING IS NOT EDITING

Editing fixes the surface — commas, typos, a clumsy word. Revision, literally “re-seeing,” changes the substance: a thesis that finally says what you mean, a paragraph moved so the logic flows, a counterargument made fairer, a weak quote swapped for a stronger one. Most writers want to skip straight to editing because it feels productive and safe. Resist that for one full pass. Read your draft asking not “is this correct?” but “is this convincing, and is it really what I think?” Edit last, once the argument is settled.

📋 A THREE-LEVEL REVISION CHECKLIST
Level 1 — The whole argument
☐ Can a reader find your thesis in the first paragraph, stated as a claim?
☐ Does every body paragraph support that one thesis? (Cut or fix any that wander.)
☐ Did you actually answer the counterargument, or just mention it?
Level 2 — The paragraphs
☐ Does each paragraph open with a point of your own, not a quote?
☐ Is every quotation framed and explained — with your explanation longer than the quote?
☐ Do the paragraphs connect? (This is where conjunctive adverbs from Lesson 7.4 earn their place.)
Level 3 — The sentences (edit last)
☐ Semicolons, colons, and quotation marks correct (Lessons 6.4, 7.4, and the colon week)?
☐ Any sentence you can’t read aloud in one breath — can it be split?
☐ Quotations copied exactly? Each tradition named respectfully, in its own terms?
✅ PART 2 · REVISION OR EDITING?
⚡ SORT EACH CHANGE
Is each change a revision (substance) or an edit (surface)?
1. Rewriting your thesis so it actually names the thinker you argue against.
2. Fixing a comma splice between two sentences with a semicolon.
3. Moving your counterargument paragraph earlier so your answer can build on it.
4. Replacing a vague quote with one that directly supports your point.
✏️ DO THIS NOW · ON YOUR DRAFT
Open your Week 8 draft and any feedback you received. Make one full revision pass first using Levels 1 and 2 — resist fixing commas yet. Then do a separate Level 3 editing pass. Save the result as your final version (same file name, replacing the draft) in your Module 1 folder, and submit it to the Synthesis Essay assignment. This is the version graded on the 100-point rubric.

Once your argument is settled, you will present it out loud in the VR Gallery Finale — a walking tour of your completed Hall of Thinkers. Speaking your argument is one of the best final checks there is: if you can walk someone through it, it holds together.
