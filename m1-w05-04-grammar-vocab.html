Five Words, and the Apostrophe
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Five words from this week, and the apostrophe — a tiny mark that trips up more writers than almost any other.
The apostrophe does two jobs (possession and contraction) and is famous for one trap (its vs. it’s). Get these straight and you remove one of the most common errors in student writing — the kind graders notice instantly.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to define five words from the reading and use one in a sentence of my own.
2
use the apostrophe correctly for possession and contraction, and never for a plain plural.

reckoning (n.)
a settling of accounts; a final judgment. “King on the day of reckoning”
inviolate (adj.)
never broken, harmed, or violated; sacred and untouched. “this inviolate soil”
eternal (adj.)
without beginning or end; lasting forever. “God the eternal”
compassionate (adj.)
feeling and showing sympathy and care for others’ suffering. “the Compassionate, the Merciful”
gracious (adj.)
kind, generous, and forgiving, especially from a position of power. “those to whom Thou hast been gracious”

📚 PART 2 · THE APOSTROPHE

The apostrophe has exactly two jobs.

1. Possession. Add ’s to a singular owner: the Qur’an’s opening, a soul’s power, Confucius’s sayings. For a plural that already ends in s, add just an apostrophe: the believers’ prayers.
2. Contraction. The apostrophe marks missing letters: do not → don’t; it is → it’s; cannot → can’t.
⚠️ THE TWO TRAPS
its vs. it’s. It’s means “it is” (a contraction). Its shows possession and has no apostrophe (like his and hers). Test: if you can’t swap in “it is,” use its.
Never use an apostrophe for a plain plural. “Three surahs” — not “three surah’s.”
📝 ON THE BOARD
The believers’ prayers fill the mosques each day.

💡 WHY THIS IS WORTH KNOWING

The its / it’s mix-up and the stray plural apostrophe are two of the errors readers spot fastest, and fixing them makes your writing look instantly more careful. Watch your apostrophes in this week’s Quick Write — you will be writing possessives constantly (“the Qur’an’s view,” “Genesis’s account”).
