📖 WHERE YOU ARE
Seven traditions, complete. Aristotle gave the module’s title question its most direct answer: a good life is the soul working well over a whole life — built by habit, balanced at the mean, and crowned by understanding. You now have everything you need. Starting next week you write the How to Live Well essay, in which you set these voices against your own considered answer. Today you close out Aristotle and take the first planning step.
📝 THE QUESTION YOU ARE ANSWERING
“How ought a person to live?”
📝 TODAY'S WORK · THREE STEPS
1
Submit your Quick Write. Finish your argument on virtue and habit and upload it to the Quick Write assignment in Canvas. Save a copy in your Module 1 folder.
2
Add the last section to your project document. In M1_HowToLiveWell, add a heading Aristotle. In three or four sentences, summarize his answer — happiness is the soul working well over a complete life; we become good by habit; each virtue is a mean between excess and deficiency; the highest activity is understanding. Use your Lesson 7.1–7.3 Learning Log entries.
3
Start planning the essay. Look back across all seven sections of your project document. In a new heading My Answer (draft thinking), write a few sentences: Of the seven thinkers, which two come closest to your own view of how to live — and which one do you most want to argue against? What is your one-sentence answer to the question, “How ought a person to live?” This is rough; it becomes the seed of next week’s thesis.
💡 ALSO THIS WEEK
Your two VR activities walk the golden mean as a spectrum and add the seventh and final station — for Aristotle — to your Hall of Thinkers. With all seven stations standing, your Hall is now a walkable map of the whole module, ready to use when you plan the essay. Keep the snapshots in your Module 1 folder.
⭐ ALSO TODAY · SENIOR STUDIOS (~30 MIN)
Assemble: draft your full Audience & Problem Brief from your notebook pages. The three required sections are on the assignment page in Week 8. Next Friday you polish and submit.
✅ BEFORE YOU LOG OFF

Check four things: the Quick Write is submitted, your project document has an Aristotle section, you have drafted a few sentences of your own answer, and all seven Learning Log entries are saved in your Module 1 folder. Seven traditions down — next week, you write.
