📖 WHERE YOU ARE · A NEW STREAM BEGINS
Your Senior Studios pitch deck is underway: Studio Sessions 1 and 2 launched it, and it grows a little every Friday until it comes due at the end of Week 8. Today a separate stream begins: the module project. As you were told at the start of the year, in Modules 2, 3, and 4 you choose your project from a menu of six formats. You pick one this module, and different ones in the modules to come. From now on, every Friday is a work session toward the project you choose today.
🎭 THE PROJECT MENU · SIX FORMATS
📝 Analytical / Synthesis Essay
A formal argument about the text. Claim, textual evidence, and a fair answer to the other side — the form you built all of Module 1. (Argument-driven.)
⚖️ Disputatio
A formal, structured debate of a genuine question from the novel — you argue more than one side at full strength, then judge. Written or recorded. (Argument-driven.)
📖 Journal / Diary
An in-character set of journal entries — the creature, Victor, Elizabeth — that reveals the novel from the inside, grounded in real textual moments.
🧐 VR Experience
A built VR experience that lets a visitor walk through an idea or scene from the novel and understand your interpretation of it. (Multimedia.)
🎬 Short Film / Screenplay
A short film or a screenplay scene that adapts or reimagines a moment of the novel — with choices that carry an interpretation. (Multimedia if filmed.)
🎤 Podcast / Oration
A recorded podcast episode or a delivered speech that makes an argument about the novel aloud, for an audience. (Multimedia & spoken; can be argument-driven.)
🔑 CHOOSE WITH THE WHOLE YEAR IN MIND · TWO RULES
You have three project picks across Modules 2, 3, and 4. Across those three, two requirements must be met by the end of the year:
• At least one multimedia project with a recorded spoken component (VR, short film, or podcast/oration).
• At least one argument-driven project (essay, disputatio, or oration).
You don’t have to do both this module — but plan ahead so you’re not boxed in later. Stretch yourself: pick a format you haven’t tried.
🎯 WHAT YOUR PROJECT IS ABOUT

Whatever format you choose, your project makes a point about Frankenstein (and, by the module’s end, Nietzsche) and one of the module’s big questions:

• What does a creator owe what they create?
• Who gets to count as human — and who decides? (the creature as “the other”)
• When does ambition or knowledge become dangerous?
• Is a person born good or bad, or made so by how they’re treated?
📝 TODAY'S WORK · THREE STEPS
1
Choose your format from the six above. Pick the one that fits how you best make a point — and that moves you toward meeting the two year-long rules.
2
Lock a working topic. In a new document M2_Project in your Module 2 folder, write your chosen format, the question you’ll take on, and a one-sentence working claim or angle.
3
Gather your raw material. Copy two or three quotations and your best reading-notes from Weeks 1–3 into the doc — the moments you might build on. You already have a head start.
📅 WHAT YOUR FRIDAYS WILL LOOK LIKE
Friday Project work session
Week 3 (today) Choose your format; lock a topic and working claim; gather evidence.
Week 4 Plan the whole thing — outline (essay/disputatio), storyboard (VR/film), entry list (journal), or script beats (podcast).
Week 5 Build the first full draft — write it, record it, or assemble it in VR.
Weeks 6–7 Bring in Nietzsche; deepen and revise; get and use feedback.
Weeks 8–9 Finalize and submit. You’ll get a rubric matched to your chosen format.
⚠️ YOUR WORK, WHATEVER THE FORMAT
A tool can draft an essay or a script outline in seconds — which is why the grade is for your interpretation: the claim you make about the novel, the evidence you choose, the choices behind your film or VR or podcast. Build it from your own reading. Any tool you use for mechanics or feedback does not replace your thinking or your voice.
⭐ ALSO ON FRIDAYS · SENIOR STUDIOS (~30 MIN)
Deck time: build Slide 2 (The Problem) from your Quarter 1 brief, then rough in Slide 1 (The Hook). One strong sentence per slide beats six weak ones. And this week’s skill carries over: a sharp em dash can give your app’s one-line description or tagline a little punch — like this.
✅ BEFORE YOU LOG OFF

Check three things: you have chosen a format, your M2_Project doc names your format, question, and working claim, and you have two or three quotations gathered to build on. Also submit this week’s Quick Write. Next Friday, you plan the whole project.
