The Pitch and Its Audience
This quarter your Studio work turns toward the deck. First, learn the genre you are about to write in.
🎯 TODAY'S FOCUS
Last quarter you named your investors and proved your problem was real. This quarter you build the six-slide deck that carries your case. Today you learn how the pitch works as a piece of rhetoric: what each slide does, and which of the three classical appeals it leans on.
💭 WARM-UP · NO WRONG ANSWER
Think of the last time someone convinced you to try something new (an app, a show, a food). What actually moved you?
📖 THE LESSON
An investor pitch is one of the oldest rhetorical situations wearing modern clothes: a speaker, a skeptical audience, and a case that must be made in minutes. The Greeks named the three ways a case moves an audience, and every strong pitch uses all three. Ethos is credibility: the audience's sense that you know your problem, tell the truth about your evidence, and can be trusted with their money. Logos is reason: the numbers, the demonstration, the logic that holds when someone pushes on it. Pathos is feeling: the story that makes an abstract problem belong to a real person. A pitch that is all logos is a spreadsheet read aloud; all pathos, a commercial; all ethos, a resume. The craft is in the mix, and the six-slide sequence distributes the mix for you. This is also where Module 2 and your Studio work shake hands: Victor Frankenstein makes something extraordinary and never once asks who it serves or who must be persuaded to welcome it. You are learning, in miniature, the discipline he skipped.
📊 THE SIX SLIDES, AND THE APPEAL EACH ONE LEANS ON
Slide Its job Leans on
1 · The HookThirty seconds that earn the room’s attentionPathos, with ethos in your composure
2 · The ProblemWho has it, how real, how widespread (your Q1 brief lives here)Logos, warmed by pathos
3 · The AppOne plain sentence; what the AI does, without jargonLogos, and ethos in the plainness
4 · Why It WorksEvidence a non-specialist can followLogos
5 · The StoryOne user’s experience, before and afterPathos
6 · The AskWhat you want and what the investor receivesEthos: only claims you can defend
🧠 CHECK YOUR READ · UNGRADED
A student’s Why It Works slide says: “Everyone who tries the app loves it. My whole family said they would use it every day, and my little brother said it was the best thing I have ever made.” What is the real weakness an investor would see?
📝 DO THIS NOW · IN YOUR STUDIO NOTEBOOK (~15 MIN)
Under a heading called Deck Plan, list your six slides. For each, write one sentence of what yours will say and name the appeal it leans on. Where a slide has nothing to say yet (most will), write the question you need to answer instead. This plan is the skeleton you flesh out on Fridays; the finished deck is due as your Quarter 2 Studio assignment. Also this week: pick your Quarter 2 independent-reading book from the approved menu (see the Independent Reading page in Start Here; never a re-read) and write your first log line.
