📖 WHERE YOU ARE
Six traditions now. Plato shifts the ground: where the others mostly told you how to live, Plato first asks why be good at all (the Ring of Gyges) and then argues that the goal of a life is to climb toward the truth and carry it back to others (the Cave). One tradition remains — Aristotle, Plato’s own student — and then you write the essay.
📝 TODAY'S WORK · THREE STEPS
1
Submit your Quick Write. Finish your argument on Glaucon’s challenge and upload it to the Quick Write assignment in Canvas. Save a copy in your Module 1 folder.
2
Add to your project document. In M1_HowToLiveWell, add a heading Plato. In three or four sentences, summarize Plato’s answer — justice is worth choosing for its own sake, and the good life is the hard climb from shadows toward truth, then back to help others see. Use your Learning Log entries.
3
Connect. Add a few sentences: Plato says we should be just even when we could get away with not being. Which earlier text gives the strongest reason to agree with him — and does any text in this module give a reason to doubt him?
