Claude in the Studio
Three narrow, honest uses of Claude for pitch work, and how to lay your deck out in VR.
🎯 TODAY'S FOCUS
You set Claude up at the start of the year and learned the four habits: ask well, log it, use it lean, verify everything. Today you point those habits at the pitch. In Studio work, Claude has exactly three jobs. It never writes a slide, never scripts a sentence of your pitch, and never supplies a number you have not verified.
🤖 CLAUDE'S THREE JOBS ON YOUR PITCH
1
Audience research. Ask what investors in your app’s space care about, what has been funded, what has failed. Treat every answer as a lead to verify, not a fact to repeat.
2
Message testing. Paste a slide’s draft text and ask it to react as a specific skeptic: “You are a cautious investor who has seen a hundred student apps. What is the first question you would ask about this slide? Do not rewrite it.”
3
Concision checks. A deck has almost no room for words. Ask Claude to flag, never to fix: “Which words on this slide could be cut without losing meaning? Just list them.” Then make the cuts yourself.
[Skeptic’s ear]  Use on every slide before it is final. “React as a skeptical investor: what is the first question you would ask about this slide? One question only. Do not rewrite anything.”
Prompts that earn their keep go in the prompt library in your Studio Notebook, and every working session gets a line in your AI-Use Log.

🥽 YOUR DECK IN VR
Once your Deck Plan has a sentence for each slide, take it into your personal VR space. Choose any Location that helps you think, place six boards or panels in a row, and put one slide’s worth of content on each. Walking the line of boards shows you what a screen cannot: where the argument jumps, which slide is carrying too many words, and whether the story sits in the right place. Rearrange until the walk feels inevitable. There is nothing to submit today; this space becomes your rehearsal room in Quarter 3.
Facilitator note: students workshop solo in a Location of their choice. Common sticking points: overloading boards with text (suggest one sentence per board) and skipping the walk-through (the rearranging is where the learning happens).
📝 DO THIS NOW · LAUNCH THE DECK (~20 MIN)
Open PowerPoint from your school Microsoft account and create LastName_ENG4_Q2_PitchDeck_Draft1.pptx in your English IV — Senior Studios folder. Make six slides, give each its title from your Deck Plan, and put your one planned sentence on each. Ugly is fine; this is the skeleton you will flesh out on Fridays. The finished draft is due as your Quarter 2 Studio assignment in Week 8.
