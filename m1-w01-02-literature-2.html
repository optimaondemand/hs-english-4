📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In Lesson 1.1, Krishna argued that the self does not die. Here he uses that claim to answer a practical question: how should a person act?
This is the Gita’s best-known teaching: do your duty without acting for the reward. As you read, track how Krishna gets from a claim about what is true (the soul does not die) to a claim about what to do (act without attachment to results).

▶  Read the passage — The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter II (Krishna on action)
This Life within all living things, my Prince!
Hides beyond harm; scorn thou to suffer, then,
For that which cannot suffer. Do thy part!
Be mindful of thy name, and tremble not!
5Nought better can betide a martial soul
Than lawful war; happy the warrior
To whom comes joy of battle—comes, as now,
Glorious and fair, unsought; opening for him
A gateway unto Heav'n. But, if thou shunn'st
10This honourable field—a Kshattriya—
If, knowing thy duty and thy task, thou bidd'st
Duty and task go by—that shall be sin!
And those to come shall speak thee infamy
From age to age; but infamy is worse
15For men of noble blood to bear than death!
The chiefs upon their battle-chariots
Will deem 'twas fear that drove thee from the fray.
Of those who held thee mighty-souled the scorn
Thou must abide, while all thine enemies
20Will scatter bitter speech of thee, to mock
The valour which thou hadst; what fate could fall
More grievously than this? Either—being killed—
Thou wilt win Swarga's safety, or—alive
And victor—thou wilt reign an earthly king.
25Therefore, arise, thou Son of Kunti! brace
Thine arm for conflict, nerve thy heart to meet—
As things alike to thee—pleasure or pain,
Profit or ruin, victory or defeat:
So minded, gird thee to the fight, for so
30Thou shalt not sin!
Thus far I speak to thee
As from the "Sankhya"—unspiritually—
Hear now the deeper teaching of the Yog,
Which holding, understanding, thou shalt burst
35Thy Karmabandh, the bondage of wrought deeds.
Here shall no end be hindered, no hope marred,
No loss be feared: faith—yea, a little faith—
Shall save thee from the anguish of thy dread.
Here, Glory of the Kurus! shines one rule—
40One steadfast rule—while shifting souls have laws
Many and hard. Specious, but wrongful deem
The speech of those ill-taught ones who extol
The letter of their Vedas, saying, "This
Is all we have, or need;" being weak at heart
45With wants, seekers of Heaven: which comes—they say—
As "fruit of good deeds done;" promising men
Much profit in new births for works of faith;
In various rites abounding; following whereon
Large merit shall accrue towards wealth and power;
50Albeit, who wealth and power do most desire
Least fixity of soul have such, least hold
On heavenly meditation. Much these teach,
From Veds, concerning the "three qualities;"
But thou, be free of the "three qualities,"
55Free of the "pairs of opposites,"[FN#2] and free
From that sad righteousness which calculates;
Self-ruled, Arjuna! simple, satisfied![FN#3]
Look! like as when a tank pours water forth
To suit all needs, so do these Brahmans draw
60Text for all wants from tank of Holy Writ.
But thou, want not! ask not! Find full reward
Of doing right in right! Let right deeds be
Thy motive, not the fruit which comes from them.
And live in action! Labour! Make thine acts
65Thy piety, casting all self aside,
Contemning gain and merit; equable
In good or evil: equability
Is Yog, is piety!
Yet, the right act
70Is less, far less, than the right-thinking mind.
Seek refuge in thy soul; have there thy heaven!
Scorn them that follow virtue for her gifts!
The mind of pure devotion—even here—
Casts equally aside good deeds and bad,
75Passing above them. Unto pure devotion
Devote thyself: with perfect meditation
Comes perfect act, and the right-hearted rise—
More certainly because they seek no gain—
Forth from the bands of body, step by step,
80To highest seats of bliss. When thy firm soul
Hath shaken off those tangled oracles
Which ignorantly guide, then shall it soar
To high neglect of what's denied or said,
This way or that way, in doctrinal writ.
85Troubled no longer by the priestly lore,
Safe shall it live, and sure; steadfastly bent
On meditation. This is Yog—and Peace!
From The Song Celestial, or Bhagavad-Gîtâ, translated from the Sanskrit by Sir Edwin Arnold (1885). Public domain.
📚 PART 1 · FROM THE SOUL TO ACTION
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
When you do something difficult and right but it goes unrewarded — no credit, no win, no thanks — how does that usually sit with you? (No wrong answer.)

In the previous lesson, Krishna argued that the self does not die. Here he uses that claim to answer a practical question: how should Arjuna act? His reasoning is straightforward. If the outcomes of action — winning, losing, even dying — cannot change what a person fundamentally is, then Arjuna should do his duty for its own sake rather than for what it might gain him.

📜 FROM THE TEXT · AS IT WAS WRITTEN
But thou, want not! ask not! Find full reward
Of doing right in right! Let right deeds be
Thy motive, not the fruit which comes from them.
And live in action! Labour! Make thine acts
Thy piety, casting all self aside…

Krishna’s point is not that results do not exist, but that they should not be the motive. He is describing a kind of discipline: act with full effort, and stay steady — equable — about whether the action succeeds or fails.

📚 PART 2 · TWO WAYS TO READ IT

This teaching can be read in more than one way, and careful readers disagree. Read one way, it offers relief: if you do what is right and let go of the outcome, you are freed from the anxiety and disappointment that come with chasing results. Read another way, it is troubling: in this scene Krishna is using the teaching to convince Arjuna to fight a war against his own relatives, and detachment from outcomes can make it easier to do something harmful. The poem does not settle this. It gives the relieving reading in Krishna’s own voice and leaves the harder question to the reader. It is also worth remembering what Krishna wants here: he is not a neutral teacher but someone trying to move Arjuna from refusal to action.

▶  How readers have answered (read after you write)
Readers who find it freeing point to the relief it offers: do what is right, release the outcome, and you are no longer ruled by anxiety about results. Readers who find it troubling point to the situation: the teaching is being used to send Arjuna into a war against his own family, and detachment from outcomes can quiet a conscience that maybe should not be quiet. Both readings have support in the text. Your job is to decide which you find stronger, and why.

💡 ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

Notice the shape of Krishna’s argument: first a claim about what is true (the soul does not die), then a claim about what to do (act without attachment). Many strong arguments move this way — from a premise to its practical consequence — and yours can too. The question of how to act when you cannot control the result will return later this year in Stoic writing and in stories about endurance.
