📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The Qur’an says the human being was made of the “goodliest fabric” — and can sink to “the lowest of the low.” Both at once.
Today’s two short passages put a high view of human dignity right next to a sober view of human failure and limits. As you read, watch how a single sentence can hold both, and notice the condition that decides which way a person goes.

📚 PART 1 · HIGH AND LOW IN ONE BREATH
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Is a human being basically noble, basically flawed, or both at once? (No wrong answer.)

Look at the central sentence: “That of goodliest fabric we created man, then brought him down to be the lowest of the low.” In a single breath it states a high view and a low one. The human being is made of the finest material — and can fall to the very bottom. This is a pairing of opposites, what writers call antithesis, and the contrast does something a single claim could not: it captures the full range a person can travel. Then comes the hinge word: “Save” — meaning “except.” Except for “who believe and do the things that are right.” The passage does not leave the outcome to chance; it names a condition. What a person becomes — goodliest or lowest — turns on how they live.

📚 PART 2 · NEVER MORE THAN YOU CAN BEAR

The second passage adds a claim about what Allah asks of that human being: “We will not burden a soul beyond its power… and they shall not be wronged.” It cuts two ways. It is a statement about Allah — that the demands placed on a person are just, never crushing, never unfair. And it is a statement about the human being — that a person has real power, real capacity, enough to carry what they are given. Read alongside Part 1, a picture comes together: humans are capable of the highest and the lowest; they are never asked for more than they can manage; and so what they make of themselves genuinely rests on them. Set this beside Genesis from Week 2, where the human was both “image of God” and “dust” — two traditions reaching for the same double truth about what a person is.
