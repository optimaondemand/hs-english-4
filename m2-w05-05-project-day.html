Lesson 5.5 · Project Day
Build the First Full Draft
Friday · turn your plan into a complete first version
📖 WHERE YOU ARE
You’ve finished the novel, and last Friday you built a plan. Today you make a complete first draft — start to finish, even if it’s rough. A whole rough draft is far more useful than a perfect introduction and nothing else: you can’t revise what doesn’t exist yet. Aim to get the entire thing down today.
📝 TODAY'S WORK · DRAFT END TO END
1
Work straight through your plan, section by section (or scene by scene, entry by entry). Don’t stop to polish — if a part is hard, leave a note in brackets and keep moving.
2
Put your evidence in place. Drop in the quotations you gathered, with the page or chapter, so they’re ready to explain. (For film/VR/podcast, place the lines or images each beat uses.)
3
Reach the end. Write a rough close, conclusion, or final scene — even one sentence. A finished-rough draft is today’s goal.
🧭 DRAFTING TIPS BY FORMAT
Essay / Disputatio
Write the body paragraphs first, then the intro — it’s easier once you see your real argument. Integrate quotes with a signal phrase and your own explanation.
Journal / Diary
Draft every entry, even briefly. Keep the voice consistent and tie each entry to a real moment in the text.
VR / Short Film
Block out every scene/station now, rough is fine — placeholders for visuals you’ll refine. Write the narration or script lines in full.
Podcast / Oration
Write the full script — you’ll record next. Mark the quotation you’ll read aloud and where you’ll pause for emphasis.
⚖️ USING CLAUDE JUDICIOUSLY
Claude can help you get unstuck — rephrase a clumsy sentence, suggest a transition, or check whether your argument flows — but the draft must be your writing and your ideas. A good test: could you explain, out loud, why every sentence is there? If you use Claude, note where, and never paste in text you couldn’t have written and defended yourself.
⭐ ALSO ON FRIDAYS · SENIOR STUDIOS (~30 MIN)
Deck time: build Slide 5 (The Story): one user, before and after. Your Quarter 2 Recitation is also open this week; what it teaches about how a speech turns will serve this slide. And this week’s skill carries over: sentence variety sells a story — open with a crisp, short line, then let a longer one explain it. Short sentences land.
✅ BEFORE YOU LOG OFF

Check three things: your draft runs start to finish, your evidence is in place, and you’ve left yourself bracketed notes on what to fix. Also submit this week’s synthesis Quick Write. Next module brings Nietzsche; over the coming weeks you’ll deepen the project with his ideas, then revise and finish it.
