Five Words, and Parallel Structure

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Five words to keep from this week’s reading, and one of the most useful tools in good writing: parallel structure.
You already heard parallel structure at work this week — the Beatitudes (“Blessed are X: for they shall Y”) repeat the same grammatical shape again and again. Today you will learn what that tool is, why it makes writing clearer and stronger, and how to fix it when it breaks.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to define five words from the reading and use one in a sentence of my own.
2
recognize parallel structure, explain why it helps, and fix a sentence whose parallelism is broken.

📚 PART 2 · PARALLEL STRUCTURE

Parallel structure means putting ideas of equal importance into the same grammatical form. When you list, compare, or repeat, the parts should match: all nouns, or all verbs ending the same way, or all phrases shaped alike. The Beatitudes are a clear model — every line is “Blessed are [noun phrase]: for they shall [verb phrase].” The matching shape is what makes the passage feel ordered and memorable, and it is what lets the meaning land. Parallel structure does the same thing in ordinary writing: it makes lists and comparisons easy to follow and gives sentences a clear rhythm.

Parallelism breaks when one item in a series does not match the others. That is a common error, and it makes a sentence stumble:

⚠️ FAULTY PARALLELISM
Broken: The Gita values steadiness, duty, and being calm.
Two nouns and then a phrase — the third item does not match.
Fixed: The Gita values steadiness, duty, and calm.  (three nouns — all matching)
📝 ON THE BOARD
The Gita values steadiness, duty, and calm.

💡 WHY THIS IS WORTH KNOWING

Parallel structure shows up everywhere strong writing makes a list or a comparison — thesis statements, résumé bullet points, the three reasons in an essay. Matching forms make your meaning easy to follow and give your sentences force. Try to build one clearly parallel sentence into this week’s Quick Write.
