📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Four lines that Muslims regard as containing the heart of the entire Qur’an — the absolute oneness of Allah.
Sura 112, “The Unity,” is among the shortest chapters in the Qur’an, yet it is one of the most recited. As you read it, notice how much of it is stated as negation — saying what Allah is not — and ask why a writer might define something most important by what it is not.

▶  Read the surah — Sura 112, “The Unity” (Al-Ikhlas)
In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.

God the eternal!
He begetteth not, and He is not begotten;

The Qur’an, Sura 112 (Al-Ikhlas, “The Unity”). Public domain English text.
📚 PART 1 · ONE, AND ONLY ONE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Is it easier to describe something huge by saying what it is, or by saying what it is not? (No wrong answer.)

The surah opens with a command — “Say” — which is worth noticing: the text is not only describing Allah but instructing the believer to declare this. What is to be declared is oneness: “He is God alone: God the eternal.” This is the center of the Islamic understanding of Allah — that Allah is one, undivided, with no partner and no equal. Everything else in the surah serves that single idea. In four short lines the Qur’an states the belief a Muslim returns to more than almost any other.

📚 PART 2 · DEFINING BY NEGATION

Look at how the surah fills out that oneness: “He begetteth not, and He is not begotten; and there is none like unto Him.” These are negations — statements of what Allah is not. Allah has no parent and no child; Allah is in no family line; nothing whatever resembles Allah. Why teach the most important thing this way? Because positive descriptions risk shrinking Allah down to something we can already picture — and the surah’s whole point is that nothing we can picture will do. By ruling out every comparison (“none like unto Him”), the lines push the reader past every familiar image toward something genuinely beyond them. It is a technique you will meet again: when a subject is too large for direct description, writers often define it by what it is not.
