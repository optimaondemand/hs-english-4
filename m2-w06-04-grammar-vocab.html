Concision: Cut Until It’s Sharp
Saying more by writing less
🎯 WHAT THIS LESSON IS FOR
Nietzsche wrote in aphorisms — short, dense lines that pack a whole argument into a sentence (“Man is a rope… over an abyss”). You don’t have to write like him, but you can borrow the discipline: concision, the habit of cutting every word that isn’t doing work. Tight writing reads as confident and lands harder — in an essay, a script, a podcast, or a one-line app pitch.
✏️ PART 1 · GRAMMAR: CUT THE DEAD WEIGHT

Notice it first. Same idea, two lengths:

WORDY (29 words)
Due to the fact that the creature was completely and utterly rejected by basically everyone that he happened to meet, he eventually ended up turning to revenge.
TIGHT (12 words)
Rejected by everyone he met, the creature turned to revenge.

Name the usual culprits. Concision means trimming words that add length but not meaning: wordy openers (“due to the fact that” → “because”), redundancy (“completely and utterly,” “ended up turning”), filler intensifiers (“basically,” “really,” “very”), and hedges (“happened to,” “sort of”). The test: delete a word; if the meaning survives, leave it out.

⚡ USE IT · TIGHTEN IT
Pick the tighter version that keeps the meaning; you’ll get an instant note.

3. Which keeps only words that work?

Concision is not the same as short. A long sentence can be tight if every word earns its place; a short one can still be padded. Cut the dead weight, then keep what carries meaning.

📚 PART 2 · VOCABULARY FROM THE READING

Five words from this week’s Nietzsche.

surpass (verb)
to go beyond; exceed. “Man is something that is to be surpassed.”
abyss (noun)
a deep, bottomless chasm. Man is “a rope over an abyss.”
nihilism (noun)
the belief that life has no meaning or value — the danger Nietzsche feared if values lost their footing.
conjure (verb)
to call upon earnestly; summon. “I conjure you… remain true to the earth.”
succumb (verb)
to give way or be overcome. “Love thy virtues,—for thou wilt succumb by them.”
aphorism (noun)
a short, pointed saying that captures a truth. Nietzsche wrote in aphorisms.
✏️ WRITTEN PRACTICE · COPIA + CUT

Start from this padded sentence:

“Due to the fact that Nietzsche basically thought that human beings are the kind of creatures that have the ability to surpass themselves, he really did conjure his readers to not ever succumb to nihilism.”

In your reading notes: (1) cut it down to a tight sentence with no dead weight (aim for under 20 words); then (2) write one aphorism of your own — a single sharp sentence — about a theme from this module. Use at least two of this week’s vocabulary words across your work.

💡 CARRY IT INTO YOUR PROJECT

As you build your project this week, run a concision pass on anything a reader or listener will actually receive — your thesis, your narration, your script, your app’s tagline. Cut the filler. The fewer dead words, the more your real ideas show.
