Five Words, and Punctuating Quotations

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Five words from the parables, and the rules for punctuating a quotation — which you need every time you put someone else’s words in your writing.
Last week the colon helped you introduce a quotation. This week you learn how to punctuate the quotation itself: where the commas and periods go, when to capitalize, and how to attach a speaker. The parables, full of dialogue, are a perfect model.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to define five words from the reading and use one in a sentence of my own.
2
punctuate a quotation correctly — commas and periods, capitalization, and speaker tags.

a gentle warning or piece of firm advice. “patient of admonition” (willing to be corrected)

📚 PART 2 · PUNCTUATING A QUOTATION

When you put someone’s exact words on the page, a few rules keep it clean. Here they are, with examples from this week:

📝 ON THE BOARD
“An elephant is like a fan,” said the blind man.
1. A comma joins a speaker tag to the quote. The elephant thought, “I owe my life to these carpenters.”
2. Periods and commas go inside the closing quotation mark. “An elephant is like a fan,” said the blind man.
3. Capitalize the first word of a quoted full sentence. The Buddha said, “In weeping over the death of dear ones…”
4. If you weave a quote into your own sentence, no comma is needed. The blind men each insist the elephant is “like a pillar” or “like a fan.”

Rule 4 is the one you will use most in essays. When you quote just a phrase and it fits the grammar of your own sentence, you do not stop it with a comma — you let it flow. Combine this with last week’s colon (to introduce a full-sentence quotation after a complete sentence) and you can handle nearly every quotation an essay will ask of you.

💡 WHY THIS IS WORTH KNOWING

Quoting accurately and punctuating it cleanly is the single most common technical task in literary writing — and a frequent place to lose easy points. You have used quotations in every Quick Write so far; now you can punctuate them with confidence. This week’s Quick Write is a parable with dialogue, so you will get to use these rules right away.
