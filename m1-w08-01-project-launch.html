The Module Project: Your Own Answer
Seven traditions are in. Now you write what you think.
🎯 WHAT THIS LESSON IS FOR
This lesson launches the two-week module project. By the end of it you will know exactly what you are building, how the two parts fit together, and where to find the material you already wrote — so that when you start drafting in the next lesson, you are working from your own seven weeks of thinking, not from a blank page.
📖 THE QUESTION YOU’VE BEEN CIRCLING
📝 ON THE BOARD
How ought a person to live?

Every text in this module gave an answer. The Gita: act rightly without clinging to the reward. The Bible: love, and depend on something beyond yourself. Confucius: become a cultivated person through steady practice. The Buddhist parables: loosen the grip of craving that causes suffering. The Qur’an: live in submission and gratitude. Plato: climb from shadows toward truth, and be just for its own sake. Aristotle: build virtue by habit and let the soul work well across a whole life. They do not agree. Your job now is not to pick the “right” one — it is to weigh them and stake out your own considered answer.

🧩 TWO PARTS, OVER TWO WEEKS
✍️
1. The Synthesis Essay — the heart of the project
A formal essay (about 900–1,200 words) in which you answer the question, draw on at least four of the seven traditions, and argue your view against the strongest opposing one. You draft it this week and finalize it next week.
🧐
2. The VR Gallery Finale — meet the thinkers
In your completed Hall of Thinkers — all seven stations standing — you record a short walking tour that presents your answer, station by station, citing who you agree with and who you argue against. It doubles as a speaking rehearsal for the essay.
👉 AND THEN, NEXT MODULE
The question does not close when the essay is submitted. It opens the next module: the reading turns to Frankenstein and Nietzsche, where the question becomes what a maker owes the people a creation is for. Your Senior Studios work turns with it: the problem you proved real in your Audience & Problem Brief becomes the pitch deck you build across Quarter 2. Keep your essay and Learning Logs; you will build on them.
📁 YOU ALREADY HAVE THE RAW MATERIAL
Open your Module 1 folder. You should find seven Learning Log entries (one per thinker), seven Quick Writes, and your M1_HowToLiveWell project document with a section on each tradition plus your draft thinking. That is your evidence bank. You are not starting over — you are pulling together work you have been building for seven weeks.

⚡ FIRST INSTINCT · NO WRONG ANSWER
Before you plan anything: if you had to answer right now, in one phrase, which thinker’s view of the good life is closest to your own?
