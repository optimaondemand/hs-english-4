Four Habits That Make AI Useful and Honest
Ask well, keep records, use it lean, and verify everything.
✍️ HABIT 1 · WRITE A GOOD PROMPT
A vague request gets a vague, generic answer. A specific one gets something you can actually use. A strong prompt usually has four parts:
ROLE / CONTEXT
Who you are and what you are doing. “I am a 12th grader comparing the Gita and Aristotle on duty.”
THE TASK
Exactly what you want it to do. “Explain this sentence,” not “help with Plato.”
THE MATERIAL
Give it the actual passage or your actual notes, so it works from your reading, not a guess.
THE LIMITS
What you do NOT want. “Do not rewrite it; just point to the problem.” “Two sentences, plain words.”
WEAK:  Help me with my pitch.
STRONG: I am a senior building an investor pitch for an app that helps new students find study groups. Here is my one-sentence description. Ask me three questions an investor would ask about the PROBLEM it solves. Do not suggest slides or wording.
🧠 CHECK THE HABIT · UNGRADED
You want help with a hard passage in Plato’s cave allegory. Which prompt will get the most useful answer?
📝 HABIT 2 · KEEP A RECORD (THE AI-USE LOG)
Whenever you use Claude on graded work, keep a short, honest log. It protects you (it shows the work is yours) and it makes you notice how you are actually using the tool. Four columns are enough:
What I asked What it gave me What I kept What was wrong
“Who invests in education apps, and what do they look for?” A list of investor types and four things they weigh Two audience insights, reworded into my brief It named a “report” I could not find; I dropped that claim
Keep the log in your Learning Log, in a section named AI-Use Log. Studio assignments and projects that invite Claude will ask for it.
⚡ HABIT 3 · USE IT LEAN
AI tools measure work in tokens, small chunks of text (roughly a few characters each). Every word you send and every word it sends back costs tokens, and your account has limits. You do not need to count them, but a few habits keep you from running out mid-project:
  • Send only what is needed. Paste the paragraph you are working on, not the whole book.
  • Start a fresh conversation for a new task. Long, wandering chats carry all their earlier text along and cost more each turn.
  • Ask for the length you want. “Two sentences” costs far less than an unbounded answer, and is usually more useful anyway.
  • Do the easy thinking yourself. Save the tool for the moment you are genuinely stuck.
🔍 HABIT 4 · VERIFY EVERYTHING
This is the most important habit. AI tools sometimes state false things with total confidence. The classic trap in an English class: you ask for a quotation and it hands you a line that is not in the text, worded just plausibly enough to fool you. It can also get a date, an author, a statistic, or a source wrong. In your Studio work the same trap looks like an invented market figure or a “study” that does not exist.
⚠️ THE RULE
Never put an AI-supplied quotation, date, statistic, or fact into your work until you have confirmed it against the real source with your own eyes. A quote is only yours to use once you have found it in the text yourself; a number is only yours to pitch once you can say where it comes from.
📚 ONE MORE HABIT WORTH BUILDING · THE PROMPT LIBRARY
When you find a prompt that works, it becomes a tool you will use again, so do not throw it away. Keep a prompt library: a short running list of your best prompts, each with a label and a note on what it is for. Yours will grow all year as your pitch develops.
[Objection finder]  Use before revising an argument. “Here is my claim and my evidence. List the three strongest objections a skeptical reader would raise. Do not soften them.”
Keep the library at the bottom of your Learning Log or in its own page of your Senior Studios document.
💾 SAVE WHAT YOU KEEP TO ONEDRIVE
Anything you decide to keep, save into your English IV folders in OneDrive, named the usual way for this course (for example LastName_ENG4_Q1_AudienceNotes.docx). A chat window is not storage; if you close it, the conversation can be gone. Your OneDrive folder is where your real, submittable work lives.
💪 CARRY THIS FORWARD
Ask well, log honestly, use it lean, verify everything, and save what you keep. Do those five things and Claude will make you faster without making the work any less yours. Each assignment will tell you exactly where it fits; the thinking stays with you.
