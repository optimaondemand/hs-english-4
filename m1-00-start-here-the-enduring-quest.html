Welcome to English IV
The Enduring Quest
Senior year asks the largest questions there are. This course gives you a year to answer them in your own voice.
📖 WHAT THIS YEAR IS ABOUT
This course returns to the oldest motif in literature, the quest, and asks the questions every quest is really about: What endures when everything else falls away? What does it mean to be human? How do we flourish? You will put those questions to some of the most demanding texts ever written, from the Bhagavad Gita and the Book of Psalms to Frankenstein, Nietzsche, Conrad, and Kafka. None of them agree with each other. By the end of the year, the position that matters is yours.
🗺️ THE YEAR AT A GLANCE
QUARTER 1 · MODULE 1
Ancient Wisdom
Seven traditions on how to live: the Bhagavad Gita, the Bible, the Confucian Analects, Buddhist parables, the Qur’an, Plato, and Aristotle.
Project: the “How to Live Well” synthesis essay + a VR gallery of thinkers.
QUARTER 2 · MODULE 2
The Creature and the Creator
Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein, then Nietzsche’s challenge: who decides what is good?
Project: your choice from a six-format menu (essay, disputatio, film, VR, podcast, journal).
QUARTER 3 · MODULE 3
Into the Dark
Conrad’s Heart of Darkness, Marcus Aurelius, Emerson, and poems by Hopkins, Housman, and Donne.
QUARTER 4 · MODULE 4
What We Become
Kafka’s The Metamorphosis, with poems by Herbert, Blake, and Dickinson.
⭐ RUNNING ALL YEAR · SENIOR STUDIOS
Alongside the reading, every senior carries one approved AI-app concept through their core courses. In English, that concept becomes The Pitch: a Shark Tank-style investor pitch you build all year, about thirty minutes a week, mostly on Fridays. It is required, it is graded, and it ends in a six-slide deck and a delivered three-minute pitch. The Senior Studios Briefing page (setup step 2) explains the whole program.
💡 HOW EACH WEEK WORKS
Three literature lessons (close reading of the week’s text), one grammar & vocabulary lesson, and a Friday Project Day that closes the week: you submit the week’s Quick Write (assigned Monday, due Friday), add to your module project, and give your Senior Studios pitch its thirty minutes. Two short VR activities run alongside. Once each quarter you will also record a short recitation of a passage you have studied closely, and your independent reading (one choice book per quarter, from the menu you know) moves at whatever pace suits you.

📝 DO THIS BEFORE LESSON 1.1 · EIGHT STEPS
1
Read this page. You just did most of it. Know the four modules and the weekly rhythm before you start.
2
Read the Senior Studios Briefing. It explains the year-long pitch, the four steps it is built in, and what is due this semester.
3
Set up Claude. Work through the two Working with Claude pages. The first gets the Claude app running with your Optima account; the second teaches you to prompt well, keep records, and verify what it gives you. You will use Claude as an assistant on your Studio work and projects, never as a replacement for your own thinking.
4
Build your OneDrive workspace. The Module 1 Setup page walks you through your Module 1 folder, your Learning Log, your project document, and your year-long Senior Studios folder.
5
Submit the Setup Check. One screenshot and two sentences that tell your teacher you are ready.
6
Pick your independent reading book. The Independent Reading page carries the approved menu you know from past years. One book you choose, each quarter, at your own pace, with the lightest possible accountability. Senior rule: never a re-read.
7
Take the Module 1 Intro Check. A short, ungraded survey: your first guess at what it means to live well, before any of the reading.
8
Begin Lesson 1.1. The Bhagavad Gita is waiting, and it opens on a battlefield.
