📖 WHERE YOU ARE
Last Friday you chose your project format and locked a topic. Today you turn that into a plan — the blueprint you’ll build from next week. A good plan now saves you from a messy draft later, whatever format you picked. You have plenty of material: three weeks of reading notes, two Quick Writes, and this week’s argument about Victor’s choice.
📝 TODAY'S WORK · THREE STEPS
1
Sharpen your claim or angle. In M2_Project, rewrite your working claim into one clear sentence that says exactly what your project will argue, show, or explore.
2
Build the plan for your format (see the guide below). This is the structure you’ll fill in next week.
3
Attach your evidence. Under each part of your plan, drop in the quotations or scenes from the novel you’ll use there.
🧭 PLAN BY FORMAT
Essay / Disputatio
Outline it: thesis, then one section per reason, then the counterargument, then the conclusion. For a disputatio, list the strongest case for each side before your verdict.
Journal / Diary
List your entries: whose voice, which moments of the novel each entry covers, and what it reveals that the narration doesn’t. Aim for a clear arc across the entries.
VR Experience / Short Film
Storyboard it: list each scene or station in order, what the visitor/viewer sees, and the one idea each beat is meant to land. Note what you’ll build or shoot.
Podcast / Oration
Outline the talk: hook, your claim, two or three movements with evidence, a counterpoint, and a close. Mark where a quotation will be read aloud.
⚖️ USING CLAUDE JUDICIOUSLY
You can use your Claude account to help you plan — for example, to pressure-test your outline (“what’s the weakest part of this argument?”) or brainstorm scene ideas. Keep the judgment yours: the claim, the structure, and the evidence are your decisions. If you use Claude, note where, and make sure you can explain every choice in your plan.
⭐ ALSO ON FRIDAYS · SENIOR STUDIOS (~30 MIN)
Deck time: build Slide 3 (The App) and Slide 4 (Why It Works): the app in one plain sentence, and evidence a non-specialist can follow, verified with your own eyes. This week’s skill carries over: active voice makes Slide 3 say plainly who it helps and what it does for them — “the app reminds caregivers,” not “reminders are provided.”
✅ BEFORE YOU LOG OFF

Check three things: M2_Project has a sharp one-sentence claim, a plan in your format, and evidence attached to each part. Then submit this week’s Quick Write. Next Friday you build your first full draft.
